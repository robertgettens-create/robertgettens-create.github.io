About

A Structural Engineer at Heart

ChangHoon Lee is a professor in the Civil Engineering department at Western New England University, where they have spent years doing what they love most: getting their hands on concrete and helping students understand why structures stand. Their expertise spans reinforced concrete design, structural mechanics, and the science behind what makes materials durable under load — and the mix itself, which they approach with the same attention a chef brings to a recipe.

Ask them what they do, and the answer is simple: they're a concrete guy. Not by accident — by conviction. There is something honest about concrete. It is heavy and slow, and when you get the mix right, it outlasts everything around it. Lee brings that same unhurried precision to the classroom, where the goal is always the same: build understanding that holds.

Teaching

Courses

CE 301

Structural Analysis

A rigorous treatment of statically determinate and indeterminate structures — beams, trusses, frames — using classical and matrix methods. Emphasis on engineering judgment alongside analytical skill: knowing not just how a structure behaves, but why.

ENGR 101

Introduction to Engineering

A first-year survey that introduces students to the engineering profession, problem-solving methodology, and hands-on design. A course Lee finds as energizing to teach as any upper-division subject — the enthusiasm in the room is unmatched.

Research

Research Interests

High-Performance Concrete Mixes

Developing formulations that push the limits of compressive strength, workability, and long-term durability — including the art of knowing what goes in and why.

Structural Health Monitoring

Applying sensor networks and data analysis to detect deterioration and damage in aging concrete infrastructure before it becomes critical.

Seismic Behavior of RC Structures

Studying how reinforced concrete buildings and bridges respond to seismic loading, and how design details shape ductility and collapse prevention.

Sustainable Construction Materials

Investigating supplementary cementitious materials — fly ash, slag, silica fume — as partial cement replacements to reduce the carbon footprint of concrete construction.
