About

A librarian who teaches, builds, and keeps track.

Mike Mannheim is the librarian for the College of Pharmacy at Western New England University, where he works alongside student pharmacists from their first literature search to their last capstone citation.

The best part of the job, he’ll tell you, is the students. Teaching in the pharmacy drug information course means being in the room for the moment a question stops being an assignment and starts being a real clinical problem — where the evidence is thin, the sources disagree, and someone has to decide what the literature actually supports.

That’s the skill he is after: not finding an answer, but knowing how to build one, defend it, and find your way back to it a year later.

Research & Practice Interests

What I work on

Four threads that keep running into each other: how student pharmacists learn to evaluate evidence, and how anyone keeps what they have learned findable ten years later.

01

Drug information & evidence appraisal

How student pharmacists move from a clinical question to a defensible answer — search strategy, source hierarchy, and the judgment calls that live in between.

02

Personal knowledge management

Linked, plain-text note systems as a working method for practitioners: capturing what you read, connecting it, and being able to retrieve it under pressure.

03

Information literacy in the professional curriculum

Embedding librarian instruction where it actually lands — inside pharmacy coursework, tied to real assignments, rather than as a one-off orientation session.

04

Point-of-care resources & collection strategy

Evaluating the tertiary and point-of-care tools pharmacy students will carry into practice, and building collections that hold up after graduation.

The Whole Person

Four interests, one way of thinking.

People are usually surprised that these belong to the same person. They shouldn’t be. Every one of them is about the same thing: build a structure sound enough that you can improvise inside it.

The craft at work

Knowledge management in Obsidian

This is the thing Mike is genuinely expert at. A linked, plain-text vault built over years — notes that connect, survive software changes, and answer a question he first asked in 2019. He teaches it, tunes it, and will happily show you the graph.

The craft at home

Woodworking

Joinery is knowledge management you can hold. Measure twice, plan the whole piece before the first cut, and accept that the strongest part of the work is the part nobody sees.

Sunday mornings

Formula 1

A sport decided by information: tire data, weather models, and a strategy call made in four seconds with incomplete evidence. Which, if you teach drug information, sounds awfully familiar.

On the turntable

Jazz

The clearest argument he knows for the whole philosophy: you learn the changes cold so that you are free to leave them. Structure is not the opposite of improvisation — it is what makes improvisation possible.

You do not have to pick one.

The message Mike most wants students to take away has very little to do with databases. It is that a career and a life are not a zero-sum trade. You can be the person who knows the literature cold and the person with sawdust on their sleeves, who knows the pit-stop delta, who has opinions about Coltrane.

The interests are not distractions from the work. They are where the instincts come from — patience, structure, attention, and the confidence to improvise once you actually know the material.
