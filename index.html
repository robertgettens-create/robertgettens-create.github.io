About

A librarian who teaches, builds, and keeps track.

Mike Mannheim is the librarian for the College of Pharmacy at Western New England University, where he works alongside student pharmacists from their first literature search to their last capstone citation.

The best part of the job, he’ll tell you, is the students. Teaching in the pharmacy drug information course means being in the room for the moment a question stops being an assignment and starts being a real clinical problem — where the evidence is thin, the sources disagree, and someone has to decide what the literature actually supports.

That’s the skill he is after: not finding an answer, but knowing how to build one, defend it, and find your way back to it a year later.

Research & Practice Interests

What I work on

Four threads that keep running into each other: how student pharmacists learn to evaluate evidence, and how anyone keeps what they have learned findable ten years later.

01

Drug information & evidence appraisal

How student pharmacists move from a clinical question to a defensible answer — search strategy, source hierarchy, and the judgment calls that live in between.

02

Personal knowledge management

Linked, plain-text note systems as a working method for practitioners: capturing what you read, connecting it, and being able to retrieve it under pressure.

03

Information literacy in the professional curriculum

Embedding librarian instruction where it actually lands — inside pharmacy coursework, tied to real assignments, rather than as a one-off orientation session.

04

Point-of-care resources & collection strategy

Evaluating the tertiary and point-of-care tools pharmacy students will carry into practice, and building collections that hold up after graduation.
